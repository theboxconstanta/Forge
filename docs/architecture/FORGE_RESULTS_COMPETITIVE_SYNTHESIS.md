# Forge Results Domain — Competitive Research & Synthesis

Competitive research and synthesis only. This document does not design the Results domain architecture — it builds the evidence base that `RESULTS_DOMAIN_ARCHITECTURE.md` v1.0 will be designed against, exactly as `FORGE_PROGRAMMING_COMPETITIVE_SYNTHESIS.md` served that role for Programming. Every finding here is either (a) direct evidence from a competitor's own documentation, product behavior, or public technical discussion, or (b) a direct citation of a finding already established in `RESULTS_DOMAIN_ASSESSMENT.md` (forge-admin-web root, commit `f7522fe`). Where evidence is thin or second-hand, that is disclosed explicitly rather than smoothed over.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Seven platforms were researched — btwb, SugarWOD, Wodify, PushPress, Kilo, TrainHeroic, TeamBuildr — spanning CrossFit-native results tracking (btwb, SugarWOD), general gym-management-with-results (Wodify, PushPress, Kilo), and strength/performance-sport tracking (TrainHeroic, TeamBuildr). No two platforms solve the Results domain identically, but the divergence is not random: it clusters around one question — **what happens to a logged result when the Programming it was scored against later changes or disappears** — and Forge's own assessment already found the worst-evidenced answer to that question living in its own schema today (`wod_logs`/`skill_logs` FK to `wods` `ON DELETE CASCADE`).

The strongest, most consistent industry signal is that **automatic PR detection and first-class benchmark/named-workout identity are not optional features — they are the load-bearing mechanisms that make a results system feel intelligent rather than clerical.** Forge currently has neither wired into its primary logging path (`RESULTS_DOMAIN_ASSESSMENT.md` §Biggest Finding: logging the day's main WOD never creates a PR, even for a recognized Hero WOD). Every CrossFit-native competitor researched treats this as baseline, not differentiation.

The second-strongest signal is that **unit handling is a genuine industry-wide weakness** — no platform researched has clearly documented, unambiguous canonical-unit storage with safe display conversion, and at least one (Wodify) has a real, currently-live defect where a single leaderboard can display mixed units per entry. Forge's own `personal_records` table already does this correctly (real unit column, real conversion); `wod_logs`/`skill_logs` do not. This is a rare case where Forge is already ahead of the field on one table and behind on two others — a genuine, low-risk differentiation opportunity if unified.

This synthesis does not recommend an architecture. It establishes, with evidence, what the industry has already learned — including what it has learned the hard way — so that the architecture document that follows can make deliberate choices instead of accidental ones.

## 2. Research Methodology

Each platform was researched independently via parallel background agents instructed to ground findings in official help documentation, product behavior descriptions, public technical discussion (Reddit, gym-owner forums, vendor blogs), and — where available — direct product screenshots or walkthroughs, and to explicitly flag marketing-only claims as unverified rather than presenting them as confirmed behavior. Agents were instructed to cite sources and disclose uncertainty rather than fabricate coverage.

Coverage was uneven by necessity, not oversight:

- **btwb, Wodify, PushPress, SugarWOD** (CrossFit-native or CrossFit-serving competitors) had the richest public documentation and yielded high-confidence findings across most of the 10 required dimensions.
- **TrainHeroic, TeamBuildr** (strength/performance-sport platforms without a native CrossFit metcon model) yielded high-confidence findings on PR/analytics/coupling but required explicit acknowledgment that entire dimensions (benchmark model, scaling representation in the CrossFit sense) do not map cleanly onto their domain — this mismatch is itself a finding, not a research gap.
- **Kilo** yielded a single, decisive finding — its gym-management product has no native Results engine and federates entirely to a third party (WodUp) — after which further research on the other 9 dimensions was not applicable.
- **SugarWOD** required working around a genuine TLS failure on `help.sugarwod.com` mid-research (Wayback Machine and cached marketing pages substituted). Two claims sourced this way are explicitly flagged lower-confidence in Section 6.

Where a finding rests on a single source, a search-synthesized page, or an unverifiable marketing claim, this document says so inline rather than presenting all findings at uniform confidence.

## 3. Common Industry Patterns

Patterns confirmed independently across a majority of the researched platforms, treated as near-consensus:

1. **Automatic PR detection is the norm, not the exception.** btwb, Wodify, TrainHeroic, TeamBuildr, and SugarWOD all auto-detect PRs to varying degrees; manual PR entry exists everywhere as a supplementary path, never the primary one. Forge's current primary WOD-logging path has neither — the only automatic path is Skill Work, and even that requires a manual confirm step (`RESULTS_DOMAIN_ASSESSMENT.md`, `computeSetsPrCandidates`).

2. **Rx/Scaled is the universal default scaling binary among CrossFit-native platforms.** btwb, PushPress, Wodify, and SugarWOD all default to a binary Rx/Scaled field at the leaderboard level; none has a structured, leaderboard-native equivalent of Forge's own 4-level RX/Intermediate/Beginner/On-Ramp model. Richer differentiation, where it exists, is achieved through custom gym-configured divisions (PushPress) or free-text elaboration (SugarWOD's "Scaled Option 1/2"), not a second structured field.

3. **Named/benchmark workout identity is the prerequisite for good PR and history tracking, not an optional nicety.** SugarWOD states this most explicitly in its own help documentation: custom, one-off workouts are excluded from PR tracking, and combining programmed components into a single logged entry breaks tracking on an otherwise-recognized named workout. btwb's benchmark categories auto-update only against its own curated taxonomy. PushPress's "Copy From Benchmark" is the mechanism that enables auto-linking. Forge's own assessment found this same coupling missing entirely — `isBenchmark` recognition against `HERO_WODS_INFO` is checked only in the standalone PR-entry screen, never against an actual `wod_logs` row.

4. **Athlete-owned, account-scoped history that survives gym departure.** Confirmed independently and with direct evidence in both btwb (documented) and SugarWOD (confirmed via a real Reddit thread) — an athlete's logged history is tied to their account, not their gym membership, and persists after they leave. This is a real cross-industry convention, not a single vendor's idiosyncrasy.

5. **Movement- and rep-scheme-specific PR granularity, not one PR per movement.** btwb tracks PRs broken down by rep count; TrainHeroic's Trophy Case names entries like "5RM Deadlift"; TeamBuildr tracks per-lift, per-tracking-type; Wodify has a weight-at-given-rep-count PR pathway. A flat "one current PR per movement" model is not what the industry converged on.

6. **Coach/owner-facing commercial analytics are structurally separate from athlete performance analytics, with little integration between the two.** Wodify's Insights dashboard is distinct from its Performance Results; PushPress's Habit Tracking is explicitly siloed from performance; SugarWOD markets an attendance-performance correlation feature that could not be verified as a real, shipped capability (see Section 6). Forge's own assessment confirms zero coach/owner analytics exist today (`RESULTS_DOMAIN_ASSESSMENT.md` §Zero coach/owner analytics).

## 4. Divergent Patterns

Real philosophical splits, not just feature-richness variance:

### 4.1 Data-permanence / history-mutation philosophy (four distinct postures found)

- **btwb — block the mutation.** A workout cannot be edited once it has logged results tied to it; the risk is prevented from occurring rather than managed after the fact.
- **PushPress — allow container deletion, never cascade to the score.** A dedicated PushPress help article states directly that deleting a Program, Track, or Division never deletes the underlying stored score record — only display and leaderboard visibility change.
- **Wodify — allow editing, retroactively reinterpret stored data, cascade on delete.** Editing a workout component after results exist can change what a stored number *means* (a Reps→Rounds scoring-type change reassigns the interpretation of already-logged values, with only an inline comment as a trace), and deleting a scored component deletes its results. This is the clearest single example of tight coupling found across all seven platforms — and it has documented real-world cost (Section 5).
- **TeamBuildr / TrainHeroic / SugarWOD — snapshot-preserve the raw entry, keep only derived values live.** TeamBuildr: "changing a tracking type will not retroactively track past data." TrainHeroic splits by time — forward-looking prescriptions stay coupled to a live working max, but logged history is frozen. SugarWOD (moderate confidence, see Section 6): scoring-configuration edits do not appear to retroactively recalculate already-logged results.

Forge's own current position — cascade-delete of `wod_logs`/`skill_logs` when a `wods` row is deleted — is worse than any of the four postures above. It is not "live-linked but preserved" (Wodify's position); it is outright destruction, with no analog in the platforms researched except as the explicit negative case PushPress's own documentation warns against avoiding.

### 4.2 Programming↔Results coupling is not one dial — it splits along different axes on different platforms

- **TeamBuildr splits by direction:** Programming→Results is tightly coupled (prescribed load auto-populates from a live working max); Results→Programming is loosely coupled (free logging doesn't feed back structurally).
- **TrainHeroic splits by time:** forward-looking percentage-based prescriptions are live-coupled to the athlete's current max; backward-looking logged history is decoupled and frozen once logged.
- **SugarWOD splits by layer:** the identity/reference layer is coupled (a named workout is required for PR/history tracking to activate at all); the mutation layer is decoupled (edits to scoring config don't cascade into logged results).
- **Wodify is uniformly, undifferentiated tightly coupled** — no split at all, in either direction, time, or layer.
- **btwb enforces independence via a hard guardrail** rather than an architectural split — it prevents the coupling risk from ever manifesting, at the cost of editing flexibility.

This variety is itself the most useful finding in this entire research effort for Forge's own decision: "coupling" is not binary. A platform can be deliberately coupled along one specific axis while remaining independent along every other axis. The failure mode to avoid is Wodify's — uniform, undifferentiated tight coupling with no chosen axis at all.

### 4.3 A genuine domain-model fork: strength-sport platforms have no metcon-native score model

TrainHeroic has no native rounds+reps composite score type; RPE/tempo/rest are free-text only. TeamBuildr has no CrossFit-style benchmark concept at all — it is replaced by "Evaluations," a combine-testing construct. Both platforms' richer PR and analytics capabilities (Section 5) are bought partly by not having to solve CrossFit's messier, more varied scoring domain — a tradeoff Forge does not get to take, since rich metcon scoring is core to its product.

### 4.4 Build vs. federate

Kilo's gym-management product does not build a Results engine at all — it federates entirely to WodUp via integration. A legitimate, real strategic alternative, though it offers Forge little direct technical guidance since Forge has already built and operates its own Results tables in production.

### 4.5 Two different philosophies for fair cross-athlete comparison

CrossFit-native platforms compare athletes within Rx/Scaled tiers (a categorical fairness model). TrainHeroic's StackUp instead uses a bodyweight-relative nonlinear ranking formula (a continuous, normalized fairness model), applied per rep-count and architecturally distinct from TrainHeroic's own separate, plain Team leaderboard — a distinction TrainHeroic's own marketing blurs but the underlying product does not.

### 4.6 Unit handling is unresolved everywhere researched

No platform has a clearly-documented, unambiguous "store canonical unit, convert for display" model. btwb's own documentation is internally inconsistent between CSV export and UI display. Wodify's leaderboard switches units per-entry to whatever the athlete entered, with no normalization — a real, currently-live defect, not a hypothetical one. SugarWOD and TeamBuildr/TrainHeroic have essentially no public documentation of storage mechanics at all. This is the one dimension where the "common industry pattern" is a shared, unresolved weakness rather than a shared solution.

## 5. Hidden Tradeoffs

1. **Automatic PR detection is only as good as movement/rep-scheme canonicalization underneath it — and competitors admit this is imperfect.** TrainHeroic's own documentation for StackUp acknowledges "sometimes bad data will get through." This is the same free-text-movement-matching problem Forge's own FCKB research already identified on the Programming side (`personal_records.movement` is free text with no canonicalization layer today) — direct competitor evidence that this is a real, not hypothetical, data-quality risk, not something automatic detection magically resolves.

2. **btwb's block-the-mutation approach buys history integrity by removing coach flexibility.** A coach cannot fix a mistake in a workout once results exist against it. A real, deliberate cost — not a free win.

3. **Wodify's allow-and-reinterpret approach buys coach flexibility by risking history integrity — and there is direct, real-world evidence the risk materializes.** Gym-owner discussion (Reddit) cites Wodify as giving limited usable historical data, with reports of lost graphs and display inconsistencies traceable to this exact retroactive-reinterpretation design. This is the one tradeoff in this research with a documented, not merely theoretical, cost.

4. **TeamBuildr and TrainHeroic's richer PR/analytics systems are subsidized by a narrower scoring domain.** They do not have to solve rounds+reps, time caps, or chippers — CrossFit's actual complexity. Forge cannot make the same trade; it needs the richer analytics without shedding the harder scoring model.

5. **Federating to a third party (Kilo→WodUp) trades control and differentiation for near-zero build cost.** A valid choice for Kilo's specific market position, but not applicable to Forge, which already owns and operates its Results tables.

6. **PushPress's gym-configurable custom scaling divisions trade leaderboard-wide consistency for gym-specific flexibility — and PushPress's own documentation discloses the real friction cost:** custom divisions require manual, per-workout enabling, with no gym-wide default. Flexibility was not free even for the vendor that built it.

7. **The near-universal silo between commercial/attendance analytics and performance analytics is a missed opportunity the industry has recognized but not solved.** SugarWOD's own marketing explicitly claims an attendance-performance correlation feature, but the research could not verify it is real, shipped product behavior rather than a roadmap aspiration dressed as a feature. That a competitor felt compelled to market this, unverifiably, is itself evidence of real, unmet demand — a genuine whitespace, discussed further in Section 10.

## 7. Forge Synthesis

This section maps the research directly onto Forge's own current state, as established in `RESULTS_DOMAIN_ASSESSMENT.md`, classifying what exists today as an asset worth preserving versus debt the industry evidence argues against.

**`wod_logs` / `skill_logs` — two separate tables with inconsistent shape.** No competitor researched has a cleanly-unified single score model that Forge should copy verbatim, but SugarWOD's single-schema, multi-set-calc-rule design and Wodify's per-component "measures" both show a unified-at-the-workout-level pattern is achievable. Forge's current split — two tables, with `variant_level` overloaded to mean different things in each flow, and `skill_logs` missing a scaling-level concept entirely — is closer to "multiple inconsistent tables" than any single competitor's model. This is architectural debt, not a defensible design choice.

**The `wods` → `wod_logs`/`skill_logs` `ON DELETE CASCADE`.** This is the single most consequential finding this research surfaces against Forge's own assessment. PushPress's own help documentation states directly that container deletion should never cascade into stored score records — only display/visibility should change. Wodify shows what happens when editing (not even deleting) a scored component is allowed to alter or remove results: real, documented gym-owner complaints about lost/unusable history. Forge's current cascade is not merely "tightly coupled" in the Wodify sense — it is strictly worse, since Wodify at least preserves *some* trace, while a `CASCADE` delete leaves nothing. No competitor researched has a worse posture on this specific question than Forge's current schema.

**Automatic PR detection wired only into Skill Work, not the main WOD log.** Every CrossFit-native competitor (btwb, SugarWOD, Wodify) treats automatic PR detection on the primary logging path as baseline. Forge's existing Skill Work confirm-step pattern (`computeSetsPrCandidates` + required confirmation) is itself a reasonable, already-shipped, already-proven design — the gap is that it was never extended to the main WOD-logging path, not that the pattern itself needs reinvention.

**Benchmark/Hero-WOD recognition decoupled from `wod_logs`.** SugarWOD's explicit doctrine — "custom workouts are not benchmarks," and using the preloaded library is required for tracking to activate — is the sharpest articulation of a pattern also visible in btwb and PushPress: a logged result needs structural knowledge of what it was scored against for the system to be useful. Forge's `isBenchmark` check existing only in a standalone PR-entry screen, never touching an actual logged `wod_logs` row, is the single largest, most competitively-validated gap this research found.

**Unit handling: a split asset.** `personal_records.unit` — a real, converted unit field — is *already ahead* of most competitors researched; this is worth explicitly naming as an asset to preserve and extend, not something needing fixing. `wod_logs`/`skill_logs` storing weight as free-text display-hints only is behind even Wodify's flawed-but-present unit storage. The fix implied by the evidence is not novel invention — it's extending Forge's own already-correct `personal_records` pattern to the other two tables.

**Zero coach/owner analytics.** Matches the near-universal industry silo (Wodify Insights vs. Performance Results; PushPress's Habit Tracking) rather than being an unusual Forge-specific gap — but SugarWOD's unverifiable marketing claim of attendance-performance correlation signals the industry recognizes this as valuable without anyone having clearly, verifiably shipped it. Combined with Forge's own real, already-working Attendance domain, this is a genuine opportunity rather than pure debt (expanded in Section 10).

**Missing indexes (`member_id`, `wod_id`, `logged_at`) beyond `gym_id` on all four Results tables.** No competitor's public documentation directly evidences their indexing strategy, but btwb's own admission that it moved its derived Fitness Level metric to a weekly batch recompute — a platform whose entire product *is* results tracking, at meaningful scale — is indirect but real evidence that naive real-time computation over unindexed data becomes a genuine constraint well before "hundreds of gyms" scale. This corroborates, rather than newly discovers, Forge's own assessment finding.

**Programming independence (`wods` has zero reverse FK from Results).** Directly validated by this research, not merely restated: TeamBuildr's and TrainHeroic's asymmetric-coupling findings both treat Programming→Results as the legitimate coupling direction and Results→Programming as the direction that must stay loose or absent. Forge's frozen principle already matches the evidence-preferred direction at the schema level — the gap is not in that principle, but in the mechanism that enforces the *lifecycle* consequences of it (the cascade-delete issue above).

## 9. Ten Non-Negotiable Results Principles

1. **A logged result, once created, must never be destroyed as a silent side effect of a Programming-domain action.** Directly addresses Forge's own `wods`-cascade-delete finding, backed by PushPress's explicit documented precedent and Wodify's cautionary, user-reported history.
2. **Automatic PR detection must be available on every logged result eligible for it, not confined to one logging flow.** Closes `RESULTS_DOMAIN_ASSESSMENT.md`'s single largest-named gap; extend the already-shipped Skill Work confirm-step pattern rather than inventing a new mechanism.
3. **Benchmark/named-workout identity must be a first-class, structurally-linked property of a logged result — never a fact the member must separately re-assert.** Matches SugarWOD's explicit doctrine and closes Forge's `isBenchmark`-decoupled-from-`wod_logs` gap.
4. **Scaling representation must be a single, consistent, structured field across every log type — never overloaded to mean different things in different flows.** Directly addresses the `variant_level` overload and `skill_logs`' missing scaling concept found in `RESULTS_DOMAIN_ASSESSMENT.md`.
5. **Unit handling must be structured and canonical everywhere a result is stored — never free-text, never display-hint-only.** Extends Forge's own already-correct `personal_records.unit` pattern; a genuine, evidenced industry whitespace, not just a catch-up fix.
6. **Results must remain athlete-owned and durable independent of gym or membership lifecycle changes.** Matches the validated, cross-platform btwb/SugarWOD convention of account-scoped, gym-departure-surviving history.
7. **Programming must never structurally depend on Results — the dependency direction is one-way, permanently.** Reaffirms Forge's own frozen principle, now independently validated by TeamBuildr's and TrainHeroic's asymmetric-coupling evidence.
8. **Where Programming and Results are coupled, the coupling must be deliberate and explicit about which axis it applies to — direction, time, or layer — never an accidental, uniform tight coupling.** Uses TeamBuildr/TrainHeroic/SugarWOD's more sophisticated coupling models as the standard to beat; Wodify's undifferentiated tight coupling is the standard to avoid.
9. **Leaderboard and analytics computation may remain read-time/computed, but must run on properly indexed, appropriately scoped queries from day one.** Directly addresses the missing-index finding; even btwb, at meaningful scale, needed a batch path for one derived metric — don't defer this as "fine for now."
10. **Coach/owner-facing performance analytics must be a real, structurally-supported capability, not an afterthought bolted onto a separate commercial-analytics surface.** Addresses Forge's own zero-analytics finding and the near-universal industry silo between commercial and performance analytics (Section 4.6/7).

## 10. Why Forge Should Deliberately Diverge

Not every finding argues for convergence with the industry consensus. Three places where the evidence points to Forge diverging deliberately, not catching up:

- **Preserve and strengthen the 4-level scaling model (RX/Intermediate/Beginner/On-Ramp), rather than collapsing toward the binary Rx/Scaled norm.** No competitor researched has a genuinely richer *structured* scaling system than what Forge already has — the binary-plus-free-text or manual-custom-division patterns found elsewhere are, if anything, less expressive. This is a place Forge is already ahead; converging downward to match the industry would be a regression, not an improvement.

- **Build real structured units everywhere, not just in `personal_records`.** This is not catching up to a competitor who already solved it — nobody researched has solved it well, and one (Wodify) has a live, documented defect from not solving it. Extending Forge's own already-correct pattern is a genuine, low-risk differentiation opportunity, not a defensive fix.

- **Deliberately connect attendance data to performance/PR data, rather than accepting the industry's commercial-vs-performance analytics silo.** SugarWOD's own marketing gestures at this (attendance-performance correlation) without verifiable evidence it is real, shipped behavior — a signal of recognized, unmet demand that no competitor researched has convincingly delivered. Forge is structurally better positioned to actually build this than any competitor studied: Forge already has Attendance, Programming, and Financial as clean, live domains on one shared schema serving multiple clients (per this session's own prior work establishing the Programming module and the platform's "one schema, multiple clients" architecture), whereas the competitors researched are either bolted-together products (Kilo+WodUp being the extreme case) or maintain siloed feature areas within a single vendor (Wodify's Insights vs. Performance Results). A true cross-domain analytics story — attendance correlated with performance, on one coherent schema — is a structural advantage Forge already has and most competitors cannot easily replicate without a significant re-architecture of their own.

- **Deliberately reject Wodify's live-linked/tightly-coupled default in favor of snapshot/protected behavior**, backed directly by real, documented user complaints found in this research — not a hypothetical risk being guarded against preemptively, but a demonstrated one.

## 11. Future Evolution

Named as forward-looking possibilities only, not scoped or designed here:

- **A btwb-style opt-in cross-gym "World" leaderboard** for specific, canonical benchmark/named workouts — the one legitimate cross-tenant comparison pattern found in this research, and one that would fit naturally once benchmark identity (Principle 3) is first-class.
- **FCKB-driven movement canonicalization applied to Results, not just Programming** — `personal_records.movement` and any future unified log schema have the identical free-text-matching weakness FCKB's own research already found on the Programming side; TrainHeroic's own admitted imperfection in automatic PR matching is direct evidence this matters in practice, not just in theory.
- **A genuinely integrated coach/owner performance-analytics surface**, built on top of Principle 10 and the cross-domain advantage described in Section 10 — explicitly the kind of capability competitors have only marketed, not verifiably shipped.
- **Richer, TeamBuildr-style comparative reporting** (e.g., an athlete-vs-gym-average view) once indexing and a unified score model (Principles 5 and 9) exist to support it efficiently.
- **Revisiting the `custom_hero_wods` vs. a unified benchmark/named-workout entity question**, flagged in `RESULTS_DOMAIN_ASSESSMENT.md` as an open question this synthesis does not resolve — Principle 3 argues for a first-class benchmark entity, but whether that subsumes, replaces, or coexists with `custom_hero_wods` is an architecture-document decision, not a synthesis one.

## 12. Final Architectural Verdict

The competitive evidence gathered here does not merely confirm `RESULTS_DOMAIN_ASSESSMENT.md`'s findings — it independently corroborates the two most severe ones (the cascade-delete risk and the missing PR-detection/benchmark-linkage on the primary logging path) against real, named competitor precedent and, in Wodify's case, real documented user harm from the closest analogous design. It also surfaces two findings the assessment could not have found on its own, since they require external comparison: that Forge's `personal_records` unit handling is already ahead of the field, and that coupling between Programming and Results is not a single dial but can be split deliberately along direction, time, or layer — a more precise vocabulary than "coupled vs. independent" for the architecture document to use.

**Verdict: this synthesis is APPROVED AS THE COMPETITIVE FOUNDATION for `RESULTS_DOMAIN_ARCHITECTURE.md` v1.0.** The next document should design against the Ten Non-Negotiable Principles in Section 9, resolve the open questions named in Section 11, and make explicit, evidenced choices on each row of the Decision Matrix in Section 8 — particularly the cascade-delete correction (Matrix #2), which the evidence in this document identifies as the single highest-priority correction relative to current production state.
