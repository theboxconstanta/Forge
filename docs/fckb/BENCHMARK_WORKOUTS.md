# FCKB — Benchmark Workouts

Covers "The Girls" (the original, canonical set of named CrossFit benchmark workouts) plus widely-used community/affiliate benchmark workouts that are NOT part of the official Hero WOD memorial list (see `HERO_WORKOUTS.md`) and are NOT official CrossFit Open workouts (see `OPEN_WORKOUTS.md`).

**Confidence note**: the workouts in this document are long-standing, extremely well-established CrossFit benchmarks whose structure has been stable and widely published for over a decade — confidence in the definitions below is high. Where a real historical or naming ambiguity exists (a few workouts are attributed differently by different sources), it's flagged explicitly rather than presented as uncontested fact.

Each entry: **Canonical Name** | Format | Structure | RX Prescription | Source/Origin Note.

---

## 1. The Girls (Original Benchmark Set)

Named female-benchmark workouts, a CrossFit tradition dating to the mid-2000s; the naming convention itself (short, then-common women's first names) was established by CrossFit founder Greg Glassman, with the stated (if informally so) analogy that these workouts, like hurricanes, "leave a path of destruction."

## 3. Structural Notes for the FCKB Schema

- Every workout in Section 1 maps cleanly to a single `workout_formats` entry from `WORKOUT_FORMATS.md` (mostly For Time and AMRAP, with Barbara's mandatory rest periods and Chelsea's EMOM being the two structural outliers within the set) — confirming the source v1.0 schema's `benchmark_workouts.workout_format_id` single-FK design DOES hold for this entire category, unlike the multi-part competition events flagged as a gap in `WORKOUT_FORMATS.md` Section 11.1.
- Linda's bodyweight-relative loading (Section 1 Note) is the one real schema stress-case in this whole document — `canonical_definition` needs to support a formula-based load prescription (`1.5 × bodyweight`) as an alternative to a fixed absolute weight, which the source schema's implied flat `canonical_definition` field doesn't obviously accommodate without a documented convention for representing formulas rather than only literal numbers.
- The Bear Complex (Section 2) is simultaneously a "benchmark workout" AND a Wave Ladder `workout_format` (WORKOUT_FORMATS.md 5.4) applied to a specific named Complex (WORKOUT_FORMATS.md 7.7) — a good illustration of `WORKOUT_FORMATS.md` Section 17's point that most real workouts are compositions, not single flat format rows, even for well-established, decade-old benchmarks, not just modern hybrid programming.

## 4. Missing/Gap Notes

The source v1.0 architecture's `benchmark_workouts` table target ("100+") is achievable and, once Hero workouts (a separate table per the source's own design) and Open workouts (also separate) are excluded as this document does, the REAL distinctly-named, widely-recognized non-Hero non-Open benchmark set is smaller than 100 — closer to the ~30 cataloged here (20 Girls + 10 widely-used community benchmarks) at a genuinely global recognition tier. Reaching 100+ requires EITHER lowering the recognition bar to include more regionally-specific or less universally-known named workouts (a legitimate choice, but one that should be made explicitly rather than implied by a target number) OR is more honestly satisfied by combining this document's ~30 with Hero (`HERO_WORKOUTS.md`, 100+ on its own) and Open (`OPEN_WORKOUTS.md`) workouts into one combined count — which is likely what the source architecture's "100+" target actually intended, given it's listed as one bullet among three separate named-workout categories rather than clearly scoped to Section 1+2 of this document alone. Flagged for clarification in `FCKB_ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW.md`.
